# 1.1 Embodied Cognition

## Definition and Core Principles

Embodied cognition represents a paradigm shift from traditional artificial intelligence approaches. Rather than viewing intelligence as abstract symbol manipulation, embodied cognition emphasizes that intelligence emerges from the dynamic interaction between an agent, its physical body, and the environment.

## Key Principles

### Intelligence Through Interaction
- Cognition is situated in the physical world
- Intelligence emerges from sensorimotor coordination
- Knowledge is grounded in physical experience

### Beyond Symbolic Processing
- Traditional AI: Logic + Search + Knowledge Bases
- Embodied AI: Perception + Action + Environment Interaction
- From "thinking about the world" to "acting in the world"

## Implications for Robotics

### Physical Embodiment Changes Everything
- Real-time constraints shape cognitive architecture
- Energy limitations affect computational strategies
- Mechanical precision influences decision-making processes

### Sensorimotor Loops
- Continuous perception-action cycles
- Closed-loop control systems
- Adaptive behavior through environmental interaction

## Theoretical Foundations

### Cognitive Science Perspectives
- Ecological psychology and direct perception
- Situated cognition and distributed intelligence
- Enactive approaches to mind and consciousness

### Philosophical Underpinnings
- Phenomenology of embodied experience
- Heidegger's "being-in-the-world"
- Merleau-Ponty's embodied perception

## Practical Implications

### Robotic System Design
- Design for physical interaction, not abstract reasoning
- Prioritize real-time performance over computational complexity
- Ground intelligence in sensory-motor experience

### Learning Approaches
- Experience-based learning through physical interaction
- Developmental robotics inspired by infant learning
- Curriculum learning through progressively complex environments
